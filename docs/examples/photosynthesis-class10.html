CBSE Class 10 · Science · Life Processes

Photosynthesis

Everything the examiner can ask about photosynthesis in Class 10 comes from a short part of the Life Processes chapter. The marks are concentrated in four places: the equation written properly, the three events in NCERT's own words, the stomata, and the experiments. This page covers those, then gives you model answers.

What you should be able to do after this
  • write the balanced equation with the conditions marked correctly;
  • state the three events of photosynthesis exactly as NCERT lists them;
  • label a leaf, a stoma and a chloroplast, and say what each part does;
  • describe the four experiments with their aim, result and conclusion;
  • answer the case-based and "give reason" questions that carry the higher marks.

1. What photosynthesis is

Photosynthesis is the process by which green plants make their own food (glucose) from carbon dioxide and water, using light energy trapped by chlorophyll, releasing oxygen as a by-product.

Plants are autotrophs — they prepare their own food. Photosynthesis supplies both the carbon and the energy the plant needs.

6CO2  +  6H2O Sunlight→Chlorophyll C6H12O6  +  6O2
carbon dioxide + water → glucose + oxygen
Writing the equation for full marks

Three things are checked: that it is balanced (6, 6, 1, 6); that sunlight and chlorophyll are written on the arrow, not added as reactants — chlorophyll is not used up, so it is a condition, not a reactant; and that you write C6H12O6, not "food" or "starch". Glucose is the product; the plant converts it to starch for storage afterwards.

2. Raw materials and conditions

What is neededWhere it comes fromHow it reaches the leaf
Carbon dioxideThe airDiffuses in through the stomata
WaterThe soilAbsorbed by roots, carried up by xylem
SunlightThe SunFalls on the leaf surface
ChlorophyllMade by the plantPresent in chloroplasts in green parts

Plants also need minerals from the soil — nitrogen, phosphorus, iron and magnesium — which they take up as ions dissolved in water. Nitrogen is needed for proteins; magnesium is part of the chlorophyll molecule itself.

A common one-mark question: why is chlorophyll not a raw material? Because it is not consumed. It traps light and is still there at the end — so it is a condition, like sunlight.

4. The three events — learn this wording

This is the highest-value thing on the page. NCERT lists three events, and board answers are marked against them:

#EventWhat it means
1Absorption of light energy by chlorophyllChlorophyll traps the sunlight falling on the leaf.
2Conversion of light energy to chemical energy, and splitting of water molecules into hydrogen and oxygenThe trapped energy is stored in chemical form, and water is split. The oxygen released goes out through the stomata. Splitting water using light is called photolysis.
3Reduction of carbon dioxide to carbohydratesCO2 is reduced — hydrogen is added to it — to build glucose.
The three-versus-four trap.

Many guide books split event 2 into two and list four events. If the question says "state the three events", write NCERT's three, keeping the splitting of water inside event 2. If you list four, you have not lost the science, but you have stopped matching the marking scheme. Safest form: write the three numbered events, and mention photolysis within event 2.

"Must these steps happen one after the other immediately?"

This exact question has appeared in a CBSE sample paper and again as a case-based passage in a recent board paper, so learn the answer.

No. The steps need not follow each other immediately. Desert plants keep their stomata closed during the day to reduce water loss. They take in carbon dioxide at night and convert it into an intermediate compound. During the day, this intermediate is acted upon by the energy absorbed by chlorophyll, and photosynthesis is completed — even though the stomata are shut.

5. Stomata and guard cells

Stomata (singular: stoma) are tiny pores, found mainly on the lower surface of the leaf. Each pore is surrounded by two bean-shaped (kidney-shaped) guard cells.

Functions

  • Exchange of gases — CO2 in, O2 out.
  • Transpiration — loss of water vapour from the leaf.

How opening and closing works

When water flows into the guard cells, they swell, become curved, and the pore opens. When the guard cells lose water, they shrink and the pore closes.

Reason question: why do stomata close when the plant is short of water? Because large amounts of water are lost through them by transpiration. Closing them when CO2 is not needed — or when water is scarce — prevents further loss. Use the switch in the figure's stoma stage to see this.

6. The experiments

Each experiment follows the same shape: destarch the plant first, set up one condition as the variable, then test for starch with iodine. Learn the conclusion sentence for each.

The starch test (used in all of them)

  1. Destarch the plant: keep it in complete darkness for 2–3 days (at least 48 hours) so stored starch is used up. Without this, a positive result proves nothing.
  2. Set up the experiment and leave the plant in sunlight for several hours.
  3. Pluck the leaf and boil it in water — this kills the cells and makes them permeable.
  4. Boil it in alcohol in a water bath — this removes the chlorophyll, so the colour change will be visible. Alcohol is heated in a water bath because it is inflammable and must not be heated directly over a flame.
  5. Wash in warm water, then add iodine solution.
  6. Blue-black = starch present. Brown / no change = no starch.

Marks are usually for the reasoning, not the recipe.

Examiners commonly ask why a step is done. The three favourites: destarching (so any starch found must have been made during the experiment), boiling in alcohol (to remove chlorophyll so the iodine colour shows), and the control (the second flask or the uncovered part, which proves the result came from your variable and nothing else).

Practice questions with model answers

Try each one on paper first, then open it. The marks shown are typical.

1 markName the pigment that absorbs light energy in plants, and say where it is found.
Model answer
Chlorophyll, present in the chloroplasts of the green parts of the plant, mainly the leaves.
1 markWhy is the leaf boiled in alcohol before adding iodine?
Model answer
To remove the chlorophyll (decolourise the leaf), so that the blue-black colour produced by iodine with starch can be seen clearly.
3 marksState the events occurring during the process of photosynthesis. Is it essential that these steps take place one after the other immediately?
Model answer

The following events occur during photosynthesis:

(i) Absorption of light energy by chlorophyll.
(ii) Conversion of light energy to chemical energy and splitting of water molecules into hydrogen and oxygen.
(iii) Reduction of carbon dioxide to carbohydrates.

No, it is not essential. These steps need not take place one after the other immediately. For example, desert plants take up carbon dioxide at night and prepare an intermediate, which is acted upon by the energy absorbed by chlorophyll during the day.

Marks: 1 for the three events (all three needed), 1 for "no", 1 for the desert-plant example. Dropping the example is the usual way students lose the third mark.

3 marksHow do guard cells regulate the opening and closing of stomatal pores? Why is this important?
Model answer
When water flows into the guard cells, they swell and become curved, which opens the stomatal pore. When the guard cells lose water, they shrink and the pore closes. This is important because large amounts of water are lost through the stomata by transpiration. The plant can therefore close them when carbon dioxide is not required for photosynthesis, or when water must be conserved.
3 marksDescribe an experiment to show that chlorophyll is necessary for photosynthesis.
Model answer
Take a potted plant with variegated leaves, such as money plant or croton, and destarch it by keeping it in a dark room for about three days. Place it in sunlight for a few hours. Pluck a variegated leaf and record which parts are green and which are white. Boil the leaf in water, then in alcohol in a water bath to remove the chlorophyll, wash it, and add iodine solution.

Observation: only the parts that were green turn blue-black; the white parts do not.
Conclusion: starch is formed only where chlorophyll was present, so chlorophyll is necessary for photosynthesis.

5 marks(a) Write the balanced equation for photosynthesis. (b) List the raw materials and state the source of each. (c) Why do desert plants open their stomata at night?
Model answer

(a) 6CO2 + 6H2O → C6H12O6 + 6O2, with sunlight and chlorophyll written above and below the arrow as the conditions.

(b) Carbon dioxide, from the air, entering through the stomata; water, from the soil, absorbed by the roots and carried up through the xylem. Sunlight and chlorophyll are also required, but as conditions rather than raw materials, since they are not used up.

(c) To reduce water loss. Desert plants live where water is scarce, and keeping stomata closed during the hot day reduces transpiration. They take in carbon dioxide at night and store it as an intermediate compound, which is used for photosynthesis during the day when sunlight is available.

Marks: 1 for a balanced equation, 1 for conditions on the arrow, 2 for raw materials with sources, 1 for the desert-plant reasoning.

Case-basedA student finds that a destarched leaf, half covered with black paper and left in sunlight, turns blue-black with iodine only on the uncovered half. (i) What does this prove? (ii) Why was the plant destarched first? (iii) What is the covered half called?
Model answer
(i) It proves that light is necessary for photosynthesis, because starch was made only where light reached the leaf.
(ii) So that all the starch already stored in the leaf was used up. Any starch detected afterwards must therefore have been made during the experiment.
(iii) The control — it is identical to the rest of the leaf in every way except that it received no light, so it isolates light as the variable being tested.

Mistakes that cost marks

MistakeWhat to write instead
Writing sunlight and chlorophyll as reactants in the equationPut them on the arrow — they are conditions, not consumed
Saying the product is "starch"The product is glucose, C6H12O6; it is later stored as starch
"Plants take in CO2 and give out O2" as the whole storyPlants also respire all the time; in daylight photosynthesis simply exceeds respiration
Saying stomata are only for photosynthesisTwo functions: gas exchange and transpiration
Describing an experiment without the controlAlways name the control and say what it rules out
Forgetting to destarch in an experiment answerIt is usually worth a mark on its own
