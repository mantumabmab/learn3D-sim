Comparative docking of etoricoxib against COX-1 and COX-2

The structural logic of coxib selectivity is unusually clean: three first-shell substitutions open a side pocket in COX-2 that does not exist in COX-1, and etoricoxib carries a methylsulfone built to occupy it. The computational experiment that seems to follow — dock the ligand into both isoforms and compare the scores — is, as designed, the single most common misuse of an empirical scoring function. These notes cover the structural basis, a protocol that is defensible, and what the comparison can legitimately support.

By the end you can
  • state the three first-shell substitutions that create the COX-2 side pocket, and what each contributes;
  • choose receptor structures and a redocking control for a ligand that has no co-crystal of its own;
  • run a reproducible Vina comparison and report it without over-claiming;
  • say precisely why a Δscore between two receptors is not a selectivity prediction, and what would be.

1. The target pair

COX-1 and COX-2 (PTGS1/PTGS2) are sequence-homologous isoenzymes, around 60% identical, with highly conserved overall architecture. Each monomer has three domains: an N-terminal EGF-like domain, a membrane-binding domain of roughly 48 residues that anchors the enzyme to one leaflet of the bilayer, and a catalytic domain carrying both the peroxidase and cyclooxygenase active sites.

The cyclooxygenase site is a long hydrophobic channel running from the membrane-binding domain into the core of the catalytic domain. Arachidonate enters from the membrane. For docking purposes the relevant geography is: a constriction near the mouth, the main channel, and an apex where catalysis occurs.

2. What makes COX-2 selectivity structurally possible

The hallmark difference is a set of first-shell substitutions in the cyclooxygenase channel. COX-1 residues Ile434, His513 and Ile523 are replaced in COX-2 by Val434, Arg513 and Val523. Together these increase the active-site volume by roughly 25% and create a side pocket off the main channel with Arg513 at its base.

Each substitution does something distinct. Val523 is the gate: losing one methyl group relative to Ile523 opens access to the side pocket, and this is the prerequisite for coxib selectivity. Ile434→Val434 lets the Phe518 side chain move back, adding further space. His513→Arg513 changes the chemistry at the base of the pocket from imidazole to guanidinium — a hydrogen-bond donor and cation that sulfones and sulfonamides can engage.

In COX-1 the bulkier Ile523 occludes the entrance, and the ligand's methylsulfone arm has nowhere to go: the clash is the structural statement of selectivity. Predict before you press: with the pocket blocked, does the rest of the molecule still fit in the main channel? It largely does — which is exactly why a docking program will still return a pose, and a score, for COX-1.

3. The ligand

Etoricoxib is 5-chloro-2-(6-methylpyridin-3-yl)-3-[4-(methylsulfonyl)phenyl]pyridine — a diaryl-heterocycle of the coxib class. Unlike celecoxib, valdecoxib and parecoxib, which carry a sulfonamide (SO2NH2), etoricoxib and rofecoxib carry a methylsulfone (SO2CH3); the oxidation state at sulfur matters for selectivity.

4. Choosing structures — and the problem you have to confront first

Before any docking, resolve this: there appears to be no deposited protein co-crystal of etoricoxib with either COX isoform. The etoricoxib crystal structures in the literature are pharmaceutical co-crystals — etoricoxib with benzoic acid, phthalic acid, picric acid — and polymorphs and hydrates, none of them protein complexes. Published docking studies of etoricoxib consequently use 5KIR or 3LN1 as the receptor. Verify this against the PDB yourself before you write it up; it is a negative claim and negative claims age.

This has a direct methodological consequence. You cannot validate your protocol by redocking etoricoxib, because there is no experimental pose to recover. You must validate against a surrogate, and the right surrogate is the closest chemotype in a structure of the same receptor.

The species trap.

Much of the COX-1 literature uses ovine COX-1 while COX-2 structures are human. If you dock into human COX-2 and ovine COX-1 and report the score difference as isoform selectivity, part of your signal is species. Either use human structures on both sides, or state the mismatch as a limitation. This is a defect in a sizeable fraction of published comparative COX docking.

What the experimental binding mode actually shows

In the 2.7 Å human COX-2–rofecoxib structure, the methylsulfone sits in the side pocket and its oxygen atoms interact with the side-chain nitrogens of His90 and Arg513 at the base of the pocket. Every other enzyme–inhibitor contact in that structure is hydrophobic. The phenyl ring extends toward Tyr385, and Val523 is what provides access. Rofecoxib binds in the same general conformation as celecoxib — the two structures overlay with a Cα r.m.s.d. of 0.31 Å over 542 pairs.

Those two hydrogen bonds are the entire polar interaction set, and that should calibrate your expectations for what a docking score is resolving here: a difference dominated by shape complementarity and buried hydrophobic surface, with two polar contacts on top.

5. A protocol that is defensible

This sandbox has AutoDock Vina 1.2.5, RDKit, Meeko and gemmi installed and working. Supply the receptor PDB files and the whole thing runs. The order below is not arbitrary: the validation step comes before the production run, and if it fails you stop.

5.1 Receptor preparation

# COX enzymes are homodimers; dock into one monomer (chain A).
# Keep the heme — it is structural, adjacent to the site, and removing it changes the pocket.
# Strip waters unless you have a specific, defended reason to keep one.
python3 - <<'PY'
import gemmi
st = gemmi.read_structure('5kir.pdb')
st.setup_entities()
st.remove_alternative_conformations()
st.remove_hydrogens()

KEEP_HET = {'HEM'}          # cofactors to retain
DROP_HET = {'RCX', 'HOH'}   # co-crystallised inhibitor + waters

model = st[0]
for ch in [c.name for c in model if c.name != 'A']:
    model.remove_chain(ch)                      # one monomer only

chain = model['A']
for i in range(len(chain) - 1, -1, -1):         # reverse: deleting shifts indices
    n = chain[i].name
    if n in DROP_HET or (chain[i].het_flag == 'H' and n not in KEEP_HET):
        del chain[i]

st.write_pdb('cox2_rec.pdb')
# sanity check: HEM present, RCX and HOH gone
print(sorted({r.name for r in gemmi.read_structure('cox2_rec.pdb')[0]['A']
              if r.het_flag == 'H'}))
PY

Save the extracted RCX residue separately before deleting it — that is your redocking reference and the source of the box centre. Check the deposited ligand code in the entry you actually download rather than trusting the one above.

Then add hydrogens at the intended pH and assign charges — reduce, PDB2PQR or Meeko's receptor path. Protonation at the side pocket is not cosmetic: Arg513 is the base of the pocket and its charge state determines whether the sulfone contact is modelled at all.

5.2 Grid box from the co-crystallised ligand

Centre the box on the centroid of the reference ligand, not on a guess. For a channel this long, a box that is too small truncates the main channel and a box that is too large wastes search effort and invites surface poses.

5.4 Validation first: redock the native ligand

# extract rofecoxib from 5KIR, prepare it, redock into the prepared receptor,
# then measure RMSD of the top pose against the crystal coordinates.
./vina --config conf.txt --ligand rofecoxib_ref.pdbqt --out rofecoxib_redock.pdbqt
The gate.

Top-pose heavy-atom RMSD ≤ 2.0 Å against the crystal pose is the conventional pass. If you do not clear it, your preparation is wrong — protonation, a retained water, the box, a missing cofactor — and nothing downstream is interpretable. Fix it and re-run. Do not proceed with a failed control and mention it in a limitations paragraph; that is not what a control is for. Report the RMSD you obtained, whatever it was.

./vina --config conf.txt --ligand etoricoxib.pdbqt --out etoricoxib_cox2.pdbqt --cpu 8

6. Reading the output honestly

Here is the part that decides whether the study is worth anything.

Vina reports kcal/mol, which invites over-reading. The score is an empirical function fitted to reproduce known binding data, not a computed free energy. Empirical scoring functions model binding as a weighted sum of steric, hydrophobic, hydrogen-bonding and torsional terms calibrated against experimental affinities, and they rarely correlate well with measured binding affinities. Vina also carries a known size- and torsion-related bias, penalising ligands with more rotatable bonds.

Comparing scores across two different receptors is the most frequent error in this exact study design.

The scoring function was fitted across a heterogeneous set of complexes; it is not calibrated to be comparable between two different proteins, and the systematic error does not cancel when you subtract. A more negative Vina score in COX-2 than in COX-1 is consistent with selectivity. It does not measure it, and the numerical difference does not convert to a selectivity ratio. Etoricoxib's reported whole-blood COX-1/COX-2 ratio is 106 in one study and 344 in another — and no docking score you obtain will distinguish those, or should be presented as if it could.

What you may legitimately claim from a well-executed run of this design:

  • that a pose is recovered in COX-2 placing the methylsulfone in the side pocket, consistent with the experimental binding mode of the rofecoxib analogue;
  • that no equivalent pose is available in COX-1, with the steric reason identified at residue 523;
  • that the protocol recovers the native pose of the reference ligand to within x Å RMSD;
  • the scores, reported as scores, with seeds, spread and the scoring function named.

What you may not claim: a predicted selectivity ratio, a binding free energy, a rank-ordering of coxibs by potency, or that the score difference explains the clinical profile.

The deeper reason structure alone will not settle it

Rofecoxib is roughly 60-fold more COX-2 selective than celecoxib, yet the two bind in the same general conformation, with the sulfone in the same pocket, in structures that overlay to 0.31 Å Cα r.m.s.d. The authors of that work attribute the selectivity difference to binding kinetics rather than any specific enzyme–inhibitor interaction. Coxib selectivity has a substantial time-dependent, slow-tight-binding component. A docking score is a single-point estimate of a static complex; it has no representation of kon, koff or a two-step mechanism. A method blind to the variable that drives the phenomenon cannot explain the phenomenon, however good the pose looks.

7. Beyond the basics: what would actually test the hypothesis

  • Receptor flexibility. Rigid-receptor docking fixes side chains that are known to move. Leu531 adopts alternate rotamers in COX-2 crystal structures, and MD identified its rotation as a way to expand the channel further. Flexible side chains, ensemble docking over MD snapshots, or induced-fit docking address what a single rigid structure cannot.
  • Rescoring. MM-GBSA or MM-PBSA on docked poses, with explicit solvation and some conformational sampling, is a better-grounded estimate than a docking score. Still not a free energy, but less naive.
  • Free-energy methods. Relative binding free-energy calculations (FEP/TI) between coxib analogues in the same receptor are the appropriate tool when you need numbers that can be compared to experiment. Absolute binding free-energy calculations now approach chemical accuracy in favourable cases.
  • Consensus and ML scoring. Consensus scoring raises pose-prediction success from roughly 66–76% for individual functions to about 80% or better; CNN-based rescoring (Gnina) raises top-1 redocking accuracy over Vina on benchmark sets. Both help pose selection more than they help affinity.
  • The right controls. Dock a selective ligand, a non-selective NSAID and a decoy set into both isoforms. If your protocol cannot separate selective from non-selective ligands across the pair, the etoricoxib result carries no weight.

Self-test

Your COX-2 redock of rofecoxib gives 0.9 Å RMSD; the COX-1 redock of ibuprofen gives 3.4 Å. Can you proceed with the comparison?

No. The COX-1 arm has failed its control, so any COX-1 score is uninterpretable, and the comparison is between a validated and an unvalidated calculation. Diagnose the COX-1 preparation first — protonation, the box, the species of the structure, a retained water in the ibuprofen site, the carboxylate's interaction with Arg120. A one-sided validation is worse than none, because it looks rigorous.

Etoricoxib scores −9.8 in COX-2 and −8.6 in COX-1. What is the strongest defensible sentence you can write?

Something close to: "Under the stated protocol (Vina 1.2.5, seed 42, exhaustiveness 32, n = 5 runs, mean ± s.d.), etoricoxib scored −9.8 ± x against COX-2 (5KIR) and −8.6 ± x against COX-1 (6Y3C); the COX-2 pose places the methylsulfone in the side pocket, reproducing the binding mode reported for rofecoxib, whereas no side-pocket pose was obtained for COX-1." You report the scores as scores and let the pose carry the structural argument. You do not write "etoricoxib binds COX-2 1.2 kcal/mol more strongly", because the function is not calibrated across receptors and a 1.2 unit gap is within the error of the method.

Why is etoricoxib having zero hydrogen-bond donors relevant to how you prepare the receptor?

Because every polar interaction in the complex has the ligand as acceptor and the protein as donor. The two experimentally observed polar contacts in the analogous complex come from the side-chain nitrogens of His90 and Arg513. If you mis-assign the protonation of either — particularly the His90 tautomer — you delete the donor and the scoring function cannot form the interaction. For this ligand, receptor protonation is not a detail; it is most of the polar signal.

A reviewer asks why you did not simply redock etoricoxib to validate. What is the answer?

That no protein co-crystal of etoricoxib exists to redock against — the etoricoxib structures in the literature are pharmaceutical co-crystals with small-molecule formers, not enzyme complexes. Validation therefore used the nearest chemotype in a structure of the same receptor: rofecoxib in 5KIR, which carries the same methylsulfone selectivity element in the same side pocket.
