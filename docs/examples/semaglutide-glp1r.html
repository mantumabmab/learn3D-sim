How semaglutide binds the GLP-1 receptor

Semaglutide is [Aib8, Arg34]GLP-1(7–37) acylated at Lys26. It engages GLP-1R by the canonical class B1 two-domain route — C-terminal helix captured by the extracellular domain, N-terminus driven into the transmembrane bundle — and the cryo-EM structure shows a binding pose closely resembling that of the native peptide. Almost everything that distinguishes the drug from the hormone is about residence, not about a different way of binding.

By the end of these notes you can
  • describe GLP-1R architecture and the two-domain binding mechanism, and state what the model does and does not predict;
  • enumerate the semaglutide–GLP-1R contacts resolved in PDB 7KI0 and say which are shared with GLP-1 and which are not;
  • explain each engineered modification in terms of the specific liability it addresses;
  • reason about why a 3-fold affinity loss was an acceptable trade for a ~165 h half-life;
  • state what remains structurally unresolved, including the fate of the C18 diacid.

1. The receptor

GLP-1R (UniProt P43220) is a class B1 (secretin-like) GPCR. Class B1 receptors are built from two modules: a large N-terminal extracellular domain (ECD) of roughly 100–160 residues, and a seven-transmembrane helical bundle. The ECD carries most of the affinity and selectivity for the peptide ligand; the TM bundle is what actually transduces the signal.

The ECD fold is conserved across the family: a three-layer α–β–β/α architecture, two pairs of antiparallel β-sheets (β1–β2, β3–β4) and an N-terminal α-helix (αA). The peptide's C-terminal segment binds as an amphipathic α-helix against a hydrophobic groove on this domain. In PDB 4ZGM — the 1.80 Å crystal structure of the semaglutide peptide backbone with the isolated GLP-1R ECD — the modelled ECD chain is 122 residues and the peptide chain 31.

2. The two-domain mechanism

The classical model for class B1 activation runs in two steps. The C-terminus of the peptide forms the initial complex with the ECD, which acts as an affinity trap: it captures the ligand from solution and raises its local concentration at the receptor, with the correct orientation. That tethering then allows the free N-terminus to insert into the juxtamembrane pocket of the TM bundle, where it drives the conformational change that opens the intracellular face for G protein coupling.

The model is well supported pharmacologically. N-terminally truncated class B1 peptides lose agonism but retain binding — they become antagonists — which is exactly what a two-site mechanism with separable affinity and efficacy determinants predicts.

Where the model is less tidy.

Human class B1 receptors differ widely in how much they actually need the ECD for activation. For CRF1R, PTH1R and PAC1R the ECD requirement can be bypassed by induced proximity and mass-action effects; GCGR and GLP-1R sit in the other group, where the ECD contribution is harder to substitute. Treat "affinity trap" as a good first-order account of GLP-1R rather than a law of the family. Note also that the semaglutide cryo-EM paper itself does not frame its findings in two-domain language; it reports the observed pose.

3. What was engineered, and why

Native GLP-1(7–37) has a plasma half-life of roughly 1.5–2 minutes. DPP-4 removes the N-terminal dipeptide His7–Ala8 by cleaving the Ala8–Glu9 bond, giving GLP-1(9–36), which is inactive at the receptor. Renal clearance removes what survives. Semaglutide carries three changes against that backbone, and Lau et al. set out four simultaneous design constraints: albumin binding to limit renal elimination, DPP-4 stability, access to the right target tissues, and retention of high receptor affinity.

The trade is explicit in the medicinal chemistry. Semaglutide's GLP-1R affinity is 0.38 ± 0.06 nM, about three-fold weaker than liraglutide, while its albumin affinity is higher. That loss is bought back many times over in exposure: plasma half-life 46.1 h in mini-pigs after i.v. dosing, mean residence time 63.6 h subcutaneously, and roughly 165 h (about one week) in humans, against 13–15 h for liraglutide. Sub-nanomolar affinity is ample; duration was the binding constraint.

4. The binding interface in 7KI0

Zhang et al. solved semaglutide–GLP-1R–Gs by cryo-EM at 2.5 Å global resolution (EMD-22882), alongside taspoglutide (7KI1). The peptide is a continuous helix: C-terminus on the ECD, N-terminus deep in the TM core, contacting TM1, TM2, TM3, TM5, TM7, ECL2 and ECL3. Most contacts reproduce the GLP-1 binding mode.

Two things worth noticing

Lys26 loses an interaction. In GLP-1- and taspoglutide-bound structures, residue 26 contacts Glu128. Semaglutide does not form that interaction — unsurprising, since Lys26 is the acylation site and its ε-amine is consumed by the linker. This is a plausible structural contributor to the modest affinity loss.

The C18 diacid was never resolved. It is absent from the consensus map. In the ECD-focused map, density for Lys26 appeared in two positions, and only the Lys26–2×OEG linker was modelled, in the downward orientation; the density then merged into the detergent micelle. The authors infer the lipid tail extends into the micelle, but the diacid itself was not modelled. Anyone drawing the acyl chain in a defined receptor pocket is drawing an inference, not a structure — including Figure 1 above, where it is shown schematically heading into the membrane.

5. From binding to signal

Insertion of the peptide N-terminus into the TM core produces the class B1 activation transition: a sharp kink and outward swing of the intracellular half of TM6, opening the cavity that receives the Gαs α5 helix. That intracellular TM6 kink and outward movement is common to the GLP-1-, semaglutide- and taspoglutide-bound consensus structures — the three peptides converge on the same active state. Coupling to Gs activates adenylyl cyclase; cAMP then acts through PKA and Epac2, and in the β-cell potentiates glucose-dependent insulin secretion.

GLP-1R also recruits β-arrestin, which drives desensitisation, endocytosis and receptor downregulation. Semaglutide shows reduced β-arrestin recruitment and reduced internalisation relative to endogenous GLP-1.

Contested.

Whether semaglutide should be called a biased agonist is not settled. Formal bias quantification across approved GLP-1R agonists has given inconsistent answers depending on the reference ligand and the methodology, and current evidence does not conclusively classify semaglutide as functionally or clinically biased. Purpose-built G protein-biased ligands that abolish β-arrestin recruitment show slower internalisation, less downregulation and longer-lasting cAMP signalling than semaglutide — which indicates semaglutide retains functionally meaningful arrestin engagement. Report it as "reduced arrestin recruitment", not "G protein-biased".

6. Beyond the basics: what the dynamics add

The consensus structures of GLP-1-, semaglutide- and taspoglutide-bound GLP-1R look similar. 3D variability analysis of the same particle sets does not.

  • Semaglutide gives the most mobile complex. The largest motions are extracellular: TM2 and ECL1 move substantially, the tops of TM1 and TM7 swing away from the core by roughly 7 Å and 8 Å, TM4/TM5 and ECL2 move outward, and the peptide itself shifts about 5 Å toward TM1/6/7 — a shift the authors link to loss of ECL2 contacts.
  • Taspoglutide is quieter at TM2/ECL1, with a peptide shift of about 3 Å; GLP-1 is quieter still, with TM1/6/7 and ECL3 relatively stable.
  • The ECD is the most dynamic element in the semaglutide complex. For taspoglutide the ECD shifts about 12 Å measured at Gly52 and for GLP-1 about 7 Å, both toward ECL1; the semaglutide and taspoglutide transition routes differ by roughly 90°.

The interpretive link worth holding loosely: ECL3 displacement has independently been identified as the structural feature that tracks with G protein bias at this receptor — N-terminally modified GLP-1R agonists that displace ECL3 outward show attenuated β-arrestin recruitment and prolonged cAMP signalling, and acetylated semaglutide behaves that way relative to semaglutide itself. Semaglutide's native dynamics already include outward movement of TM6, TM7 and ECL3 where GLP-1's do not. That is a correlation between two literatures, not a demonstrated mechanism.

Self-test

Why does replacing Lys34 with arginine matter, given that position 34 makes no critical receptor contact?

It is a chemistry constraint, not a pharmacology one. With lysines at both 26 and 34, acylation would give a mixture of regioisomers. Arg34 leaves a single free ε-amine so the fatty diacid is installed cleanly at Lys26. Arginine is the conservative swap: same charge, no free primary amine for the coupling chemistry.

A colleague's figure shows the C18 diacid docked in a defined groove on TM1. What is wrong with it?

No experimental structure supports a defined pocket. In 7KI0 the diacid is absent from the consensus map; only the Lys26–2×OEG linker was modelled, and the density merges into the detergent micelle, which is why the authors infer the tail simply extends into the lipid environment. A specific groove is an over-claim.

Semaglutide binds GLP-1R three-fold more weakly than liraglutide. Why was that acceptable?

Because affinity was not the limiting variable. At 0.38 nM the receptor is saturated at achievable exposures; the clinical problem being solved was duration. Stronger albumin binding buys a week-long half-life, and the design brief explicitly traded the two — the requirement was to maintain high affinity, not to maximise it.

What would you need to show to call semaglutide a biased agonist, and why do current data fall short?

You would need formal bias quantification against a defined reference ligand, with relative potency differences accounted for, showing a statistically significant and reproducible preference for cAMP over β-arrestin that persists across assay systems and maps onto an in vivo outcome. Published analyses disagree depending on reference and methodology, and ligands engineered to abolish arrestin recruitment outperform semaglutide on internalisation, downregulation and cAMP duration — so semaglutide clearly retains meaningful arrestin engagement.
